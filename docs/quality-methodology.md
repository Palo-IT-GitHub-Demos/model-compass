# Quality suite methodology

This page sets out how the quality prompt suite must be understood, validated
and used in this project.

## 1. Goal

The quality suite is a **generic measurement** tool that Model Compass uses to
compare models on standardised use cases.

It does not replace a client functional evaluation in
[gen-e2-eval](https://github.com/GLOBAL-PALO-IT/gen-e2-eval). Its role is to
provide the quality component of a technical recommendation and to answer the
question:

> "Which models pass the quality threshold of a generic use case?"

The catalog that maps prompts to use cases and sets the thresholds is the one of
the active suite: `data/catalog/model_compass_use_cases_v3.json` for v3
(default), `model_compass_use_cases_v2.json` for v2 and
`model_compass_use_cases.json` for v1. The v3 thresholds are calibrated on the
0.8.0 reference run (see [the scope](model-compass-scope.md)).

## 2. Origin of the prompts and how they are scored

The prompts of every suite (40 in v3, 34 in v2, 16 in v1) are **written
in-house by the project team**; they do not come from a public benchmark
dataset. Each one is attached to exactly one dimension (`quality_dimension`) and
one category (`category`) defined in
[data/prompts/quality_prompts_v3.json](https://github.com/Palo-IT-GitHub-Demos/model-compass/blob/main/data/prompts/quality_prompts_v3.json),
generated by `scripts/build_quality_suite_v3.py`.

The category sets how the answer is scored:

- if the category is registered in `CHECKS`
  ([deterministic_eval.py](https://github.com/Palo-IT-GitHub-Demos/model-compass/blob/main/src/evaluators/deterministic_eval.py)),
  the answer is scored in pure Python (JSON, exact answer, code tests in the
  sandbox), with no call to a model;
- otherwise (`generic_judgment`), the prompt must provide explicit
  `judge_criteria` and is sent to the blind judge panel (aliases A/B/C… to
  neutralise position bias), following the protocol of Zheng, Chiang, Sheng et
  al., *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena*, NeurIPS 2023 —
  [arXiv:2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), already cited in the
  [2026-08-21 audit](audit-2026-08-21.md).

Without mitigation, that study measures a strong position bias that varies by
judge: 23.8 % consistent verdicts after swapping the order of the two answers
for Claude-v1, 46.2 % for GPT-3.5, 65.0 % for GPT-4 (their Table 2; a
"consistent" verdict means that the judge keeps the same opinion once the order
is swapped). **This reference figure already includes, in their "default"
prompt, an explicit instruction such as "avoid any position bias"** —
comparable to the rule "Do NOT favour the first response you read" of our own
system prompt — and this instruction alone does not remove the bias (65 %
consistency for GPT-4, so still 35 % inconsistency).

**What this paper actually proves, and what it does not:**

- Proven: position bias is real and significant, even with an anti-bias
  instruction in the prompt.
- Proven (Table 13): replaying the judgement with the order swapped and counting
  a win only when it holds both ways ("conservative swap") is the method the
  authors actually used and measured in all their experiments.
- **Not quantified by the paper**: "assigning positions at random" is only
  mentioned as a plausible alternative ("can be effective at a large scale with
  the correct expectations"), without a dedicated results table — the authors
  state explicitly that they used the conservative approach, not this one, for
  their measurements.
- **Gap with our pipeline**: their figures concern a two-answer comparison (A vs
  B). `quality_judge.py` has **N anonymised answers scored at once** in a single
  call (see the system prompt: *"score multiple model responses"*), a setting
  this paper does not measure.

`_blind_alias_map` in `quality_judge.py` implements the "random position"
variant: the letter ↔ model mapping is reshuffled by a pseudo-random draw
specific to each prompt and attempt (seed derived from `quality_suite_id`, the
index and the text of the prompt). It is a theoretically motivated choice
consistent with the literature, **but not validated empirically in this
project**: no internal test yet measures whether our 3 judges (Claude, GPT,
Gemini) stay consistent when the same prompt is replayed with another alias
draw. Until that test is done, the mitigation must be presented as a documented
precaution, not as evidence of effectiveness.

This contract (an objective reference **or** explicit judging criteria) is
enforced at input by the `QualityPrompt` Pydantic schema — see decision **D9**
of [ADR 0001](adr/0001-architecture-initiale.md).

### Dimensions covered (suite v3)

| Dimension | Prompts | Category | Scoring |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| structured_output | 5 | json_output | deterministic (expected JSON, partial credit) |
| code_correctness | 5 | code_execution | deterministic (tests run in the sandbox, partial credit) |
| contextual_accuracy | 5 | exact_answer | deterministic (exact final answer) |
| elementary_reasoning | 5 | logical_reasoning | deterministic (exact final answer) |
| classification_accuracy | 5 | exact_answer | deterministic (exact final answer) |
| summarization_quality | 5 | generic_judgment | blind judging |
| concise_communication | 5 | generic_judgment | blind judging |
| instruction_reliability | 5 | instruction_following (4), generic_judgment (1) | 4 deterministic + 1 judged |
| output_format_compliance | derived | every `strict_output` prompt (29) | deterministic (literal compliance with the format) |

29 prompts out of 40 are therefore scored with no external judge; the other 11
follow the blind judging protocol above.

### Content and format are scored separately

A prompt marked `strict_output` produces **two rows**: its content score (is the
answer right?) in its own dimension, and a format compliance score in the
derived dimension `output_format_compliance`.

Before this split, a correct answer wrapped in ` ``` ` fences scored 1/5 on
`code_contract`: the dimension measured formatting while being read as a coding
skill. The two signals are now readable independently.

### Two scales, published separately

`avg_quality_score` aggregates two scales that are not equivalent:
deterministic checks are pass/fail rendered as **1 or 5** (with partial credit
in between for JSON and code), while judges score continuously from 1 to 5.
Since most prompts are deterministic, the overall figure is dominated by pass/fail
scores.

`avg_quality_score_deterministic` and `avg_quality_score_judged` are therefore
published side by side in the CSV and the report, to show which of the two scales
drives the ranking. Likewise, `quality_pass_rate` (share of prompts ≥ 4/5) can
be **identical for several models** — it is a coverage indicator, not a ranking.

### Prompts set aside for suspected input corruption

When **every** model of a run misses a prompt whose reference answer is known
and fixed, the shared cause is far more likely the request that actually reached
the provider (redaction by a proxy, template substitution, truncation) than a
simultaneous failure of unrelated models.

The prompt is then removed from the aggregates **and from their denominator**
(so it does not count as a coverage gap either), and counted in
`quality_excluded_prompt_count`. Detection is deliberately narrow: it only
considers prompts with a reference answer and at least two models, so that a
prompt that is merely hard, or badly formatted by everyone, is never set aside.

In line with the evidence-preservation invariant, **neither the answer nor the
raw score is rewritten**: the row stays readable in `results/quality_details/`
with `verification_status=suspected_input_corruption`.

## 3. Judge panel reliability and bias

### Judge blindness and self-preference bias

The panel has 3 Copilot judges, each backed by a provider (Claude/Anthropic,
GPT/OpenAI, Gemini/Google) that is also one of the providers that may appear in
`TARGET_MODELS`. A judge can therefore, without knowing it, score an answer
produced by a model of its own provider.

In the current panel (Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-6 Sol, Gemini 3.8 Flash, chosen among
the models enabled on the Copilot plan), the risk goes further for Google:
Gemini 3.8 Flash, the only Google model enabled on the plan, is also part of the
reference run and therefore scores, blind, its own answers. GPT-6 Sol is of the
same family as GPT-6.1 Sol, which is evaluated. The recomputation without the
same-provider judge described below removes exactly that judge for those models.

The panel is independent only if each judge really runs on the declared model.
VS Code silently runs an agent on the chat's current model when its `model:` ID
is not available. `make check-judging` therefore reads Copilot Chat's debug logs
and rejects any score file produced by another model (see
[the workflow](workflow.md), Phase 2).

**What alias blindness covers**: the part a judge reads never contains
`alias_map` or the model identifier. The judge has read access to the workspace
to read its part; `make check-judging` rejects the scores of a judge whose log
shows it reading any other file, `pending_*.json` included. The judge therefore
only sees a letter (A/B/C…) and the text of the answer (see
`.github/agents/judge-*.agent.md`, "Blind evaluation").

**What alias blindness does not cover**: an answer that introduces itself ("As
an AI developed by Anthropic…") brings back the clue the alias was meant to
hide. Since 2026-09-03, `quality_judge.py::_scrub_self_identification` removes,
on a best-effort basis, explicit self-introductions (`I'm <vendor>`,
`As <vendor>`, `developed/created/trained/made by <vendor>`) before the text
reaches the `judging_{ts}.json` file — the raw copy stays intact in
`pending_{ts}.json` for evidence and audit, in line with the "never rewrite an
answer" invariant. This detection is **best-effort, not a guarantee**: a
paraphrased or indirect disclosure is not caught.

**Published scores are never corrected**: the panel stays 3 symmetric judges and
`avg_quality_score`, the per-use-case score, the rank and the status are
computed with the 3 judges.

**Sensitivity check against the same-provider judge.** Each judge is attached to
a provider by the suffix of its identifier (`copilot-claude-anthropic` →
`anthropic`), each model by the prefix of its OpenRouter slug
(`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` → `anthropic`). For each judged answer,
`exclude_affiliated_judges` (`src/evaluators/recommendation.py`) recomputes the
mean without the verdict of the same-provider judge, if at least one other judge
remains; deterministic scores do not change. The recommendation is recomputed on
these scores and added as sidecar columns:
`quality_score_without_affiliated_judge`,
`recommendation_rank_without_affiliated_judge`,
`recommendation_status_without_affiliated_judge` and
`affiliated_judge_changes_outcome`. The report marks a use case
**judge-sensitive** when a model's status or rank changes, and names the
recommended model of that scenario.

This check does not prove that a bias exists: removing a judge also shrinks the
panel. It only shows that a recommendation depends on the judge most exposed to
the conflict of interest and must then be confirmed. If a future audit finds a
measurable systematic bias, the mitigation to consider remains a pool of judges
separate from the evaluated providers — not implemented to date.

### Judge disagreement

`judge_disagreement` (column of the main CSV and of `results/quality_details/`)
is the range (max − min) of the 3 raw scores before averaging, per (model,
prompt, attempt). `quality_judge_disagreement_rate` summarises, per model, the
share of judged prompts whose range is ≥ 2 — a disagreement of 2 points or more
on a 1-5 scale with 3 judges means the panel disagrees on the quality level of
the answer (for example 3 vs 5), not just on a rounding point (4 vs 5).

A real example (run of 2026-09-02, prompt 15): OpenAI = 3 ("lacks concrete
success criteria"), Anthropic = 4, Google = 5 ("meets all criteria") on the same
answer — mean 4.0, but `judge_disagreement = 2`. The mean alone hid this
systematic difference of severity between judges.

**Choice made: keep the mean, publish the disagreement.** The alternative
considered was to replace the mean with the median. With exactly 3 judges, the
median amounts to taking the middle judge's score and ignoring the other two
entirely — not necessarily fairer than a mean, and it would have silently
redefined every score already published.
`judge_disagreement`/`quality_judge_disagreement_rate` make the disagreement
visible without changing the historical meaning of the score.

### Anchoring the 1-5 rubric

The disagreement above came from a 1-5 rubric that was not anchored enough:
nothing stated what separates a 3 from a 4 from a 5 when an answer meets the
`judge_criteria` but a judge would, by personal preference, have liked a
different wording. Since 2026-09-03, the 3 files `.github/agents/judge-*.agent.md`
explicitly ask the judge to count how many `judge_criteria` are met before
scoring, with a decision rule based on that count (5 = all met, 3 = at least one
partly met, and so on) and an explicit ban on lowering a score for a subjective
preference absent from the listed criteria.

### Inter-judge agreement and item analysis (sidecars)

`make merge` writes two files under `results/quality_analysis/` that describe
the reliability of the quality screen without ever changing the raw scores:

- `benchmark_<ts>_judge_agreement`: Krippendorff's alpha (interval) of the panel
  and per dimension, exact and ±1-point agreement rates, mean score per judge and
  mean gap of a judge from the mean of the others (leniency/severity).
  `agreement_status = low_agreement` below alpha = 0.667 (the conventional
  "tentative conclusions" threshold). Alpha is `NaN` when all scores are
  identical.
- `benchmark_<ts>_item_analysis`: per prompt, mean, standard deviation across
  models and corrected item-total correlation (`discrimination`). Statuses:
  `saturated` (every model ≥ 4), `floor` (no model > 2), `low_variance`
  (standard deviation < 0.25), `negative_discrimination` (< −0.1),
  `insufficient_models` (< 3 models) and `suspected_input_corruption`.

A `saturated`, `floor` or `low_variance` item does not separate models: it is a
candidate for hardening or removal in the next catalog version. These statuses
exclude no prompt from the aggregates.

Short anchors for 1, 3 and 5 (`rubric-v2`) are added to the judged items and
passed to the blind batch. Their effect on inter-judge agreement is not
measured; the blind review of 50 judged answers that calibrated the 3.5
thresholds (see [the scope](model-compass-scope.md)) found the panel mean within
one point of the reviewer on 48 answers out of 50.

### Strict coverage validation before merge

Before 2026-09-03, a judge that failed to score an alias (typo, hallucinated
alias, answer simply forgotten) silently reduced the mean to 1 or 2 judges
instead of 3, without any signal. `MergePipeline._rebuild_quality_df` now
checks, for each judge whose `scores_*.json` file is loaded, that it scored 100 %
of the expected (prompt_id, attempt, alias) whose answer is not empty —
otherwise the merge fails at once (`ValueError`), naming the judge and the
missing tuples, with the instruction to rerun that judge. This is deliberately
stricter than a single malformed score (which stays ignored with a warning):
partial coverage silently changes the meaning of the mean score, an isolated
invalid value only removes one contribution.

## 3a. Suite `generic-screen-v2` and code execution

### Why v1 was not enough

Diagnosis over 12 runs and 3 models (2026-09-01 to 2026-09-04):

- 9 prompts out of 16 scored 5.00 for every model on every run: they were
  saturated and separated nothing while weighing in the coverage;
- prompts 4 and 6 ("capital of Australia", "largest planet in the solar
  system") failed because the models received `[ADDRESS]` instead of the place:
  a filter upstream of the models masks this text (see section 3b, *Prompt
  delivery*), the local text is intact. This is the
  `suspected_input_corruption` case, not a lack of capability;
- the failures of prompt 3 in the first runs came from a scoring rule older than
  the content/format split (Markdown fences counted as a content error);
- only prompt 8 (syllogism answered `NO`) reflected a real model error.

### Content of v2

`QUALITY_SUITE=v2` (or `--quality-suite v2`, repeated for `merge`) loads
`data/prompts/quality_prompts_v2.json` and
`data/catalog/model_compass_use_cases_v2.json`. v2 is an independent suite (its
own identifier and hash, `generic-screen-v1` unchanged and kept as a historical
reference). It reuses no v1 prompt and targets the priority use cases of
`docs/research/benchmark-to-use-case-mapping.md`:

| Use case | Items | Evaluator |
|---|---:|---|
| Structured extraction | 5 | exact JSON |
| Code generation | 5 | tests run in the sandbox |
| Contextual question answering | 5 | exact answer (abstention included) |
| Reasoning and analysis | 5 | exact answer |
| Classification and routing | 4 | exact answer (`UNCLEAR` included) |
| Summarization | 3 | rubric, judge panel |
| Concise communication | 3 | rubric, judge panel |
| Instruction following | 4 | exact answer, 1 rubric |

Each item declares the blueprint of ADR 0002: `item_id`, `use_case_id`,
`construct`, `task_type`, `difficulty` (easy, medium, hard) and its status
(`draft`), `language`, `evaluator`, `rubric_version` for judged items,
`license_status` and `contamination_status`. Loading fails if a field is missing
or an `item_id` is duplicated. `contamination_status = internal_unscreened`
means the items are original but not compared against public corpora. The items
avoid names of places and people, addresses and emails, which an upstream filter
can mask.

### Code sandbox contract

ADR 0002 requires a sandbox, resource limits, logs and attribution before any
generated code runs. The contract applied by `src/evaluators/code_sandbox.py`:

- **Opt-in**: nothing runs without `QUALITY_CODE_EXECUTION=true` (the default of
  the standard path). Otherwise `code_execution` answers go to the judge panel
  (criteria provided). `quality_metadata.code_execution` records what was used.
- **Before execution**: AST analysis; imports limited to an allow-list of the
  standard library, dangerous calls (`open`, `eval`, `exec`, `getattr`…) and
  dunder attributes refused. A refusal is a failure of the answer (score 1,
  explicit reason).
- **Execution**: isolated Python subprocess (`-I -S`), empty environment,
  temporary directory, 5 s timeout, CPU and file-size limits (plus memory on
  Linux), user output discarded.
- **Scoring**: `1 + 4 × share of passed tests`; the reason gives `x/y` passed
  cases. If the sandbox is unavailable, the answer goes back to the judges and
  is not counted as a model failure.
- **Logs**: code hash, function, status, passed cases, duration; never the code.
- **Accepted limit**: defence in depth, not a security boundary. The network is
  only blocked by the import allow-list and memory is not limited on macOS. Keep
  it for the answers of evaluated models.

## 3b. Suite `generic-screen-v3` (default since methodology 0.5.0)

### Why a v3

The 0.4.0 reference run (9 models, three attempts) showed that v2 no longer
separated recent models. 92 % of scores were 5/5, and 21 of the 34 prompts were
saturated: every model scored at least 4 on every attempt. Almost every eligible
model ended up statistically tied with the recommended one, and cost decided
alone. The little spread left came partly from artefacts: a correct answer shown
with its working scored as wrong (fixed since, see below), and an answer key
disputed by six models out of eight (prompt 23).

### Content

`QUALITY_SUITE=v3` (default) loads `data/prompts/quality_prompts_v3.json` and
`data/catalog/model_compass_use_cases_v3.json` (catalog `v3.2`): 40 items,
**five per use case** since methodology 0.7.0 (six before). A single failure
still does not flip eligibility at the 4.0 threshold (see
[the scope](model-compass-scope.md)).

| Use case | Items | Kept from v2 | Evaluator |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Structured extraction | 5 | 1 | expected JSON, partial credit |
| Code generation | 5 | 2 | tests in the sandbox, partial credit |
| Contextual question answering | 5 | 0 | exact final answer (abstention included) |
| Reasoning and analysis | 5 | 1 | exact final answer |
| Classification and routing | 5 | 0 | exact final answer (`UNCLEAR` under an explicit rule included) |
| Summarization | 5 | 3 | rubric, judge panel |
| Concise communication | 5 | 1 | rubric, judge panel |
| Instruction following | 5 | 1 | exact output, 1 rubric |

The items kept are those that still separated the models; saturated items are
replaced by harder variants of the same construct (a corrected value that
replaces the first one, a missing field to leave at `null`, multi-hop with an
outdated fact, an exception to an inherited rule, irony, priority between two
requests, an explicit routing rule, cumulative format constraints). The new
items favour deterministic checks: only summarization, concise communication and
one instruction-following item stay judged, 11 items since 0.7.0. As in v2, they
avoid names of people and places, addresses and emails, which an upstream filter
can mask. The expected values of the new items are computed by
`scripts/build_quality_suite_v3.py`, and each reference solution of the code
items must pass its own tests in the sandbox before anything is written. This
script is the source of the suite and its catalog: an item is changed there, not
in the JSON, and a test fails if the versioned files drift from it.

The items' `difficulty_status` field stays `draft`: changing it would change the
`quality_suite_id` fingerprint and make the reference run incomparable with the
next ones. The real difficulty is the one measured by the item analysis of that
run (`results/quality_analysis/benchmark_20261103_154602_item_analysis.csv`,
0.8.0 merge): 6 items out of 40 passed by all six models, 14 missed only by
Llama 3.3 70B, 20 that separate the leading models.

### Hardened items (methodology 0.6.0)

On the run of 2 November 2026, 30 items out of 48 were saturated for the five
leading models (a mean of at least 4.67 each), and 18 were passed by all six
models, Llama 3.3 70B included: they separated nothing. These 18 items were
replaced by new items of the same use case, under new identifiers, so that a 0.5
run is never wrongly compared with a 0.6 run:

| Use case | Removed | Added | What makes them harder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structured extraction | -05, -06, -07 | -09, -10, -11 | replaying an order log with a cancellation, values derived from a table with discounts, grouped counting under rules; partial credit per field |
| Code generation | -06, -08 | -10, -11 | version ordering with pre-releases, maximum overlap with excluded bounds; 7 to 10 tests each |
| Contextual question answering | -06 to -10 | -12 to -16 | state tracking over seven updates, an exception that overrides another, a date with an extension, abstention against a tempting estimate, counting under three conditions |
| Reasoning and analysis | -06, -08 | -11, -12 | a slot with margins around bookings, a conditional weighted mean |
| Classification and routing | -07 to -10, -12 | -13 to -17 | a batch of five messages with rule priority, scope of a notice, a rule that overrides a number, intent against keyword, abstention on a tie |
| Instruction following | -05 | -10 | filter then reverse a list |

Each expected answer is computed by the script, and a check verifies that the
right answer scores 5 and that a plausible mistake fails. The items that already
separated Llama from the other models were kept.

### Final suite: five items per use case (methodology 0.7.0)

On the run of 3 November 2026, 13 items out of 48 were still passed by all six
models, and two series of items written to be "harder" confirmed it: the five
leading models reach the ceiling on automatically scored generic tasks. Of the
18 items replaced in 0.6.0, 12 stayed passed by everyone; of the 19 candidates
tried on 3 November with `make pilot-items`, long tasks included (a 40-order
table, a 39-operation log, a batch of 24 messages, dated amendments), only one
is missed on content by a leading model, and another only on its format.

The suite therefore stops at five items per use case, chosen on evidence:

- removed: the 13 items passed by everyone, and one item that only Llama missed
  in summarization and instruction following, where six remained;
- added: the 7 candidates that at least one model missed (extraction: -15, -17,
  -18; code: -14; question answering: -17; classification: -18, -22). Those
  that only Llama misses keep a floor below eligibility.

What generic quality measures between leading models comes down to format
compliance, judged constraints (length, tone, no promise) and occasional errors:
it mainly serves as an **eligibility gate**. Separating them finely is the role
of `gen-e2-eval` on the client's data. Only one JSON item shows an example of the
expected output (-17); the other strict items do not. The candidates left out
stay in `data/prompts/quality_candidates_v3.json`, with the pilot's findings in
the build script.

### Prompt delivery (methodology 0.6.0)

The run of 2 November 2026 showed that the path to the models rewrites some
prompts, without an error and with billing: `JSON array`, `EUR` or `W1` arrived as
`[ADDRESS]`, and probes as `[PROMPT_INJECTION]`. The likely cause is an
OpenRouter workspace guardrail. A score obtained on a rewritten prompt measures
the filter, not the model.

Every `make collect` therefore first checks the text actually forwarded, through
OpenRouter's `debug.echo_upstream_body` option (16 tokens per prompt, less than a
cent for the whole path). The check has no cache: a filter that is changed or
removed is taken into account on the next run.

- **Rewritten quality item:** collection stops before any scored call. The item
  is reworded in `scripts/build_quality_suite_v3.py`, since this is a false
  positive on benign text.
- **Rewritten probe:** it is not sent and leaves the RSI (see the
  [security methodology](security-methodology.md)).
- **Check impossible:** collection stops. `PROMPT_DELIVERY_AUDIT=false`
  overrides it, and the report then flags that the run is not reference
  evidence.

### Scoring rules introduced from 0.4.0 to 0.8.0

- **Final answer.** For exact-answer items (`exact_answer`, `factual_sanity`,
  `logical_reasoning`), the content is the final answer: the last non-empty
  line, without Markdown emphasis or an "Answer:" label, and the last number of
  that line when the expected answer is numeric. A correct answer that shows its
  working keeps its content score; only format compliance fails. Since 0.8.0, two
  other positions commit to the answer when the last line does not give it: the
  **first line** if it holds only the answer (`**11:25**` then the explanation),
  unless the last line ends on another answer of the same shape; and an answer
  that the **last line** introduces with `=`, `:` or "the answer is"
  (`3/5 * 2/4 = 3/10`, "the correct answer would be NOT STATED, as…"). A value
  met in between never counts: an answer that considers the right value and then
  settles on another one fails. The merge applies this rule again to the stored
  raw answers, without changing them.
- **Correct answer outside the contract (0.7.0).** For `instruction_following`
  and JSON items, the exact output is part of what is measured: a correct answer
  buried in text is worth **3** on content, neither 5 nor 1. It is the last line
  for an `instruction_following` item (`… = 4.\n\nppln-4`), the last JSON object
  or array of the answer for a JSON item, whose partial credit then becomes
  `1 + 2 × share of correct fields`. Format compliance fails separately. Only one
  JSON item gives an example of the expected shape: the other strict items have
  none, to separate the models that only follow a format once it is shown.
- **Content only per use case.** A use case's quality score, which decides
  eligibility, averages only the content rows; format compliance is published
  apart (`format_compliance_rate`, *Format respected* column of the report).
- **Partial credit.** A JSON answer is worth `1 + 4 × share of correct fields`
  (an invented field counts as an error); code is worth
  `1 + 4 × share of passed tests`. A partly correct answer stays a failure, but
  is no longer scored like an empty answer.
- **Empty answer.** It is worth 1 on content and, if the prompt checks the
  format, 1 on format compliance too; its reason gives the API's
  `finish_reason` (`length` = budget used up without an answer). An API response
  without any `choice` is a collection error, excluded from the score.
- **Token ceiling.** `QUALITY_MAX_TOKENS` (16,384 by default, hidden reasoning
  included) bounds a model that loops up to its maximum output, like the empty
  131,072-token, 20-minute answer of the 0.4.0 run.
- **Code execution by default.** `QUALITY_CODE_EXECUTION=true` on the standard
  path: code items are scored by their tests in the sandbox, more objective and
  free, instead of by the judge panel. The allowed imports are listed in each
  prompt.
- **Item analysis on content.** Since 0.6.0, `analyze_items` ignores format
  rows: an item everyone answers correctly stays saturated, even if some models
  detail their reasoning.
- **Disputed key.** The item analysis marks `key_disputed` an exact-answer item
  whose most frequent answer, for more than half of the models, is the same
  answer the key rejects. The item is not removed: the use-case card shows the
  *disputed key* badge so that the recommendation does not unknowingly rest on a
  debatable key.

## 4. Coverage of the scope

The deterministic scoring module states its own limit:

> "The screen deliberately measures only provider-neutral fundamentals:
> structured output, factual sanity, elementary reasoning and instruction
> reliability. It is not a substitute for domain-specific task evaluation."
> — [deterministic_eval.py](https://github.com/Palo-IT-GitHub-Demos/model-compass/blob/main/src/evaluators/deterministic_eval.py)

| Capability | Status |
| --- | --- |
| format compliance | covered |
| instruction following | covered |
| question answering on a given context | covered |
| bounded reasoning | covered |
| classification and routing | covered |
| short code | covered (5 functions, tests run in the sandbox) |
| summarization and concise communication | covered (judged) |
| long context | not covered |
| business tasks | not covered |
| specialised domain evaluation | not covered |

This limit must be stated explicitly whenever the quality score is used.

## 5. Validating the suite

The quality of the suite must be validated at three levels.

### 5.1 Content validation

Each prompt must be reviewed by at least one person who knows the project, who
checks that:

- the prompt is unambiguous;
- the expected answer is correct;
- the tested capability is relevant for screening;
- the prompt introduces no artificial bias;
- the prompt stays provider-neutral.

The initial review document is
[quality-validation/review-v1.md](quality-validation/review-v1.md). A new v3
item enters the suite only after `make pilot-items LIVE=1` shows that it
separates models.

### 5.2 Technical validation (already enforced by the code)

The `QualityPrompt` schema and the pipeline guarantee, at input and at export:

- each prompt has a defined scoring type (deterministic or non-empty
  `judge_criteria`) — decision D9 of [ADR 0001](adr/0001-architecture-initiale.md);
- `avg_quality_score` is a macro-average per dimension, not a flat mean over all
  prompts — decision D10 of the same ADR;
- the suite is identified by `quality_suite_id` (hash of the prompt file), which
  ties each result to an exact version of the suite;
- coverage (`quality_coverage_rate`, `quality_dimension_coverage_rate`) and
  collection errors (`quality_collection_error_count`) are exported apart from
  the quality score.

The detail of every exported column is in [workflow.md](workflow.md) (section
*Exported quality metrics*).

### 5.3 Empirical validation (up to the run operator)

- **Repetitions**: `QUALITY_REPETITIONS=3` by default, which gives
  `quality_stability_score` and the confidence intervals. A run with
  `QUALITY_REPETITIONS=1` has neither, and the report flags it as non-reference;
- check that the scores are not all identical across models;
- compare the rankings obtained over several models;
- record the prompts that do not discriminate enough and review them first.

#### Reading `QUALITY_REPETITIONS`

For a given model and prompt, the observed success rate is:

$$
r = \frac{\text{correct attempts}}{k}
$$

- $r=1$: stable success over the observed attempts;
- $r=0$: reproducible failure over the observed attempts;
- $0<r<1$: unstable result.

The aggregate score first averages the attempts of a prompt, then the prompts of
a dimension, then the dimensions. A repetition therefore gives the prompt no more
weight in the final score. `quality_stability_score` summarises the spread
between attempts and must be read with the detailed answers.

### 95 % bootstrap intervals and sensitivity (A4/C2)

The merge publishes `avg_quality_score_ci95_low/high` in the benchmark and
`quality_score_ci95_low/high` for each use case in the recommendations. Over
2,000 reproducible draws (a local seed derived from the model, and from the use
case where relevant), the bootstrap resamples the prompts **within each
dimension**, then each prompt's attempts with replacement. It recomputes the
weighted mean per dimension, then the macro-average; for a use case, it
recomputes the flat mean of its prompts. The bounds are the 2.5 % and 97.5 %
quantiles of the draws. Published scores and ranks stay unchanged.

A use case's intervals cover content only, like its score. They need a run of at
least **3 attempts per prompt**, at least 2 attempts on each scored prompt and
full coverage of the expected prompts; otherwise the bounds are `null`. A prompt
that lost an attempt to a technical collection error is resampled on the
remaining attempts, instead of cancelling the interval of the whole model.

An empty answer is a model failure, not a collection error: it is worth 1 on
content and, if the prompt has a format check, 1 on format compliance too.
Without this second row, the model would have one format attempt fewer than the
others on that prompt. `finish_reason=length` in the reason shows that the model
used up its token budget without answering. An API response without any
`choice` is, on the other hand, a collection error: the cause is the API, not the
model. In the overall benchmark, the exclusions for suspected input corruption
are also removed from the denominator, consistent with the existing score. These
CIs reflect the resampling of this suite and these attempts, **not** a guarantee
of generalisation to business tasks. `quality_ci95_overlaps_leader` shows whether
the quality CIs overlap with at least one leader of the ranking, for eligible
models with an available CI. An overlap is not evidence of a statistical tie:
rank ties stay defined only by equal recommendation scores.

`recommendation_sensitivity_rank_min/max` and `recommendation_fragile` describe
10 deterministic scenarios per use case: each of the four effective weights
moves separately by ±10 % **relative**, with the four weights renormalised to 1;
the quality threshold also moves by ±10 % relative, bounded to the 1–5 scale. The
other weights and thresholds stay fixed in each scenario.
`recommendation_fragile=true` if eligibility or rank differs from the reference
scenario; the rank bounds ignore the scenarios where the model has no rank.
Incomplete decision evidence produces `null` for this flag, not a false
stability. Since 0.7.0, any ranked model that at least one scenario puts first
(`recommendation_sensitivity_rank_min = 1`) is `recommended` next to the leader,
with its rank unchanged: a gap that ±10 % on one weight reverses is not a
decision. This local analysis replaces neither the review of judged answers that
calibrated the 3.5 thresholds (see [the scope](model-compass-scope.md)), nor a
test of every combination of weights.

For `k=1`, a wrong objective answer carries
`verification_status=optional_openrouter_recheck`. A manual command can send two
more rechecks to the same model through OpenRouter. For `k>1`, the status
`run_repetitions_available` shows that the run's data already suffice to assess
reproducibility; the command then refuses to generate more calls and costs.

This check does not look for a culprit. Without access to the payload received
by the exact upstream and without a direct comparison with that same upstream, it
cannot separate the behaviour of the model, of the inference provider or of
OpenRouter routing. Its conclusions are deliberately limited to
`confirmed_failure`, `not_reproduced`, `unstable` and `inconclusive`.

The reports under `results/verification/` are secondary evidence: the report
links them to the benchmark, but they change neither the answer nor the score of
the historical run.

## 6. Model Compass eligibility and comparison with gen-e2-eval

For each use case of the catalog, the associated prompts are aggregated per
model. A model is quality-eligible when its coverage is complete and its mean
reaches the use case's threshold. The quality score is never lowered
artificially to offset a favourable cost or latency.

The eligible models are then ranked in `results/recommendations/` with the
following components:

- use case quality: 50 % by default;
- generic security RSI: 25 %;
- model-only cost score: 20 %;
- performance score: 5 %.

**Composite formula (corrected)**: the `quality_score` stays a mean on **1–5**
for eligibility and the CIs. For the ranking it becomes
`quality_score_100 = (quality_score - 1) × 25`, bounded to 0–100, like
`security_score` (RSI), `cost_score` and `performance_score`. With complete
evidence, the effective weights $w$ give:

$$
\text{recommendation\_score} =
w_q Q_{100} + w_s S_{100} + w_c C_{100} + w_p P_{100},
\qquad \sum w_i = 1.
$$

By default, $w_q=0.50$, $w_s=0.25$, $w_c=0.20$ and $w_p=0.05$. Cost is the cost
of 1,000 requests of the workload profile: the profile's input size at the
listed input price, plus the output length the model really produced on the
quality suite (hidden reasoning included,
`measured_completion_tokens_per_answer`) at the listed output price. A verbose
model or one that reasons at length therefore pays for its tokens. Performance
uses p95 latency. Each catalog holds their fixed logarithmic bounds (v1.2, v2.2,
v3.1 and v3.2): cost 0.05–50 USD per 1,000 requests and latency 1–30 seconds. The
score is 100 at the better bound and 0 at the worse bound, capped outside the
range; a free model gets 100 for cost. Adding or removing a model therefore does
not change the score of the models already present. Without p95 latency, the
performance evidence is missing and no tokens-per-second figure replaces it. The
weight sensitivity uses these same 0–100 components; changes of the quality
threshold still act on the 1–5 scale.

Artifacts generated before this correction wrongly used the raw 1–5 quality
score in the weighted sum: their `recommendation_score`, ranks and sensitivity
flags are **not directly comparable** with the new ones, even with the same
catalog. The raw scores and answers stay intact; recompute the recommendation
report from the evidence if a historical comparison is needed.

The weights can be replaced by `MODEL_COMPASS_WEIGHTS`, but the effective
weights and the catalog version are always written in the report. Missing
evidence produces an explicit status and no rank, rather than an optimistic
substitute value. Equal scores stay tied.

## 7. Comparison with gen-e2-eval

The experimental dialogue screen (9 two-turn scenarios, opt-in) is described in
[Multi-probe system](multiprobe-system.md). Its drift and omission rates are
**separate evidence**, not added to the single-turn scores or recommendations.
`make dialogue` is synthetic and measures no real model; `make dialogue-live`
makes paid calls.

This project's quality score and the business validation of `gen-e2-eval` are not
directly comparable, because they do not measure the same thing.

Good practice is to compare their **ability to select the same relevant
models**.

### Recommended protocol

1. run Model Compass on the same models;
2. run `gen-e2-eval` on the same models;
3. compare the top 3 and the overall rankings;
4. record the disagreements and their possible reasons;
5. keep the result in a comparison file.

The template is in
[quality-validation/gen-e2-eval-comparison-template.md](quality-validation/gen-e2-eval-comparison-template.md).

## 8. Acceptance criteria

The suite is considered properly justified if it meets at least the following
rules:

- 100 % of the prompts have a defined scoring type (deterministic or
  `judge_criteria`) — already enforced by the `QualityPrompt` schema;
- the run used for a shortlist decision has `quality_coverage_rate ≥ 0.8` and
  `quality_dimension_coverage_rate = 1.0` — thresholds already applied by the
  pipeline to enable `quality_cer_eligible` (decision D12 of
  [ADR 0001](adr/0001-architecture-initiale.md));
- the run used as the basis for an exclusion or shortlist decision was made with
  `QUALITY_REPETITIONS≥2` (3 on the standard path);
- the coverage and limits of the scope (§4) are stated next to the score in every
  deliverable;
- the generic score and the per-use-case recommendation are never read as a final
  adoption decision.
